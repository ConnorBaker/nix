N1. **Two complementary "concurrent insert-on-miss" idioms with no documented choice rubric, mixed across caches and registries.** The codebase uses two different concurrent-cache mechanisms for the same problem: `boost::concurrent_flat_map<K, V>` plus `getConcurrent`/`try_emplace_and_cvisit` (cand. #120 names the lookup half), and `Sync<std::map<K, V>>` (a coarse-grained mutex-protected map). Sites for `concurrent_flat_map`: `CachingSourceAccessor`, `MountedSourceAccessorImpl`, `WindowsSourceAccessor::cachedLstat`, `DummyStoreImpl::contents`/`derivations`/`buildTrace`, `EvalState::srcToStore`/`importResolutionCache`/`fileEvalCache`/`resolvedPaths`/`lookupPathResolved`/`positionToDocComment`, `nix_api_expr.cc::nix_refcounts`, `drvHashes` (#139), plus `aws-creds.cc` and `prim_fromTOML`'s decoded-TOML cache (sites pass 2 verified beyond pass-1's list). Sites for `Sync<std::map>`: `input-cache.cc::cache_`, `git-utils.cc::workdirInfoCache_`, `pos-table.hh::origins_`, `gc.cc::connections`, `remote-store.hh::connectionFds`, `filtering-source-accessor.cc::allowedPrefixes` (`SharedSync<std::set<...>>`).
- **Validation:** VALID — but **the pass-1 framing as "all six are insert-on-miss caches" is too broad**. Pass-2 read the cited sites end-to-end and found three of them (`gc.cc::connections`, `remote-store.hh::connectionFds`, `filtering-source-accessor.cc::allowedPrefixes`) are *registries with iteration semantics*, not memoisation caches. The proposed `Memo<K, V, Policy>` abstraction fits the cache subset; the registries need a different shape (`OrderedRegistry<T>`). **Split into N1a (insert-on-miss caches: 7+ `concurrent_flat_map` sites + 1-2 `Sync<map>` cache sites) and N1b (registries with ordered iteration: 3 sites; document why they keep `Sync<map>`).** Compounds with #120 (lookup half), #139 (`drvHashes`), #147 (file-transfer singleton). Effort: small (rubric + helper) / medium (migrate the cache-side `Sync<map>` writers).

N2. **The `<algo>:<base-encoded>` family is wider than #8 names.** Cand. #8 names three encoders for the `<algo>:<base>` shape (Hash, Signature, Key) but misses two siblings already in the tree: `Realisation::fingerprint(key)` is JSON-rendered and then signed (the resulting `<sigType>:<base64>` shape goes through `Signature` — covered by #8 — but the call site in `UnkeyedRealisation::sign` builds the fingerprint by hand each time); `ValidPathInfo::fingerprint` produces `1;<path>;<narHash>;<size>;<refs>` — a different shape but same shape-pattern (versioned-prefix + fields), used universally for binary-cache signatures; `UnkeyedRealisation::fingerprint` uses JSON-with-signatures-dropped — a parallel pattern. Plus the `<drv>!<output>` (legacy `DerivedPath::to_string_legacy`) vs modern `<drv>^<output>` separator (`to_string`/`render`) split. The right factoring is a *family* of two-three helpers: a generic `parseColonPrefixed(s, allowedAlgos, codec)` plus `formatColonPrefixed(name, codec, bytes)` shared by hash and signature; a generic `parseSeparated(s, sep)` for the `^`/`!` split; a `KeyedFingerprint<Body>` mixin (see N5) for the `1;<...>;<...>` versioned-prefix shape.
- **Validation:** VALID. Sites: `src/libutil/hash.cc`, `src/libutil/signature/local-keys.cc`, `src/libstore/path-info.cc`, `src/libstore/realisation.cc`, `src/libstore/include/nix/store/derived-path.hh`. Effort: small (formatColonPrefixed pair) / medium (full family). **See also:** #8 (extends), #102 (Keyed/Unkeyed fingerprint), #104 (Renderable), N5 (Signable mixin).

Pass-1 framed it as five-or-six; pass-2 verified the count is eight. Each has a different "duplicate registration" policy (`PrimOp` re-register silent; `Implementations::add` throws; `RegisterCommand` overwrites silent) — three drifted choices in the same logical registry pattern.
- **Validation:** VALID. **Verified count: 8 registries** (pass-2 correction over pass-1's "five"). **Latent bug** — the duplicate-registration policy varies across registries; consolidating onto one `Registry<Key, Value, DuplicatePolicy>` template forces an explicit choice per call site. **See also:** #86 (registration shape), #136 (Setting constructor self-register), #137 (`GlobalConfig::Register`), #166 (forward-decl pattern; same SIOF concerns), Cluster C, A5. Effort: medium (template) / structural (migrating all eight registrations consistently).

N5. **`Keyed<Body>` / `Signable<Body>` mixin pattern hides in three places.** Cand. #102 articulates that `UnkeyedValidPathInfo` → `ValidPathInfo`, `UnkeyedRealisation` → `Realisation`, and `UnkeyedNarInfo` → `NarInfo` form a "diamond-of-keying" pattern. Pass-2 confirms `BuildLog`'s line-buffer + JSON-frame and `LogSink`'s line-buffer + forward are the same shape with different observers; pass-1 didn't read `BuildLog` end-to-end and missed the JSON-frame case.

N8. **Async-callback / sync-future / coroutine has three parallel idioms.** The codebase has three concurrency idioms for the same problem: (1) `Callback<T>` (in `callback.hh`) — explicit continuation-passing, used in `Store::queryPathInfoUncached` and many other `*Uncached` methods, with the synchronous wrapper hand-rolled (#22); (2) `boost::asio::awaitable<T>` (the in-tree alias `nix::asio`) — used in `HttpBinaryCacheStore::topoSortPaths` via `callbackToAwaitable`/`forEachAsync` (libutil; marginal in-tree adoption); (3) `Goal::Co` (the bespoke build-system coroutine; #160) — used only by libstore/build. The same logical operation (`Store::queryPathInfo`) is exposed via *all three*. The catalog has #22 and #160 but does not name the unified question: should the codebase have one async idiom?
- **Validation:** VALID. **Recommended target:** `boost::asio::awaitable<T>` (already in tree). Three coordinated changes: (a) Convert all `*Uncached(args, Callback<T>)` to `asio::awaitable<T> *Uncached(args)`; the sync wrappers (#22) collapse to `asio::co_spawn(..., fn).get()`. (b) Convert `Goal::Co` to `asio::awaitable<T>` per #160 (keeping the bespoke `final_awaiter` per the validation note). (c) Delete `Callback<T>` once the last consumer migrates. Subsumes #22; sequences with #160 (Co → asio first); compounds with #147 (file-transfer; the curl thread is its own fourth idiom — see N17 / N43). **See also:** A2, R7, R10. Effort: structural.

N9. **Per-platform `Pid`/`Pipe`/`Process` symmetry has no shared interface.** The catalog has #27 (file-system / file-descriptor / processes per-platform parallel), #81 (Pid Unix vs Windows divergence), #163 (`Pipe::create` arity divergence), #164 (`#ifdef _WIN32` switches in headers), #161 (Windows port single-target CI lane), #162 (Windows-only public headers). All six are aspects of one observation: the platform layer has *no shared interface header*. Each header declares "one of two parallel APIs" with the choice made at preprocessor time. The current state mixes `#ifdef _WIN32` blocks in public headers, parallel `unix/` and `windows/` source dirs, platform-only headers in `windows/include/`, and a `Descriptor` typedef that's `int` on Unix and `HANDLE` on Windows — but `Pid` does *not* follow the same shape. The meta-rule "no `#ifdef _WIN32` in public headers" is missing from the catalog.
- **Validation:** VALID. Explicit "platform interface" headers (`file-descriptor-iface.hh`) carrying *only* the API; per-platform implementations include the iface and provide implementations. **Subsumes #27, #29, #81, #161, #162, #163, #164; overlaps with #152 (Worker::Waker).** Effort: structural (multi-PR; touches every libutil platform header).

N12. **The "registered fetcher" Cartesian product is over-flat — declarative attribute schemas missing.** Every concrete `InputScheme` re-implements the same flow: `inputFromURL`, `inputFromAttrs`, `allowedAttrs` (hand-written `std::map<string, AttributeInfo>`), `toURL`, `getAccessor`, `getFingerprint`. Cand. #32 (per-scheme rewrite, "BaseInputScheme<T> CRTP") is too narrowly scoped. The structural problem: `InputScheme` is *also* a registry record — it has both polymorphic runtime methods AND essentially static metadata (`schemeName`, `allowedAttrs`, `experimentalFeature`, per-key documentation). The static half should be a `consteval` descriptor; the runtime half a small set of function-pointers. This is the *same* shape as the daemon protocol (#176, #182): in both cases the table-vs-code split is wrong — the table is the source of truth and the code is an interpretation; right now both halves are written by hand.
- **Validation:** VALID. **Sites:** `src/libfetchers/include/nix/fetchers/fetchers.hh`, `src/libfetchers/git.cc`, `github.cc`, `tarball.cc`, `mercurial.cc`, `path.cc`, `indirect.cc`, plus `src/libstore/daemon.cc` (#176) and `src/libstore/remote-store.cc` (#182). An `attribute_schema<Scheme>` `constexpr` descriptor (vector of `(name, type, required, doc, getter, setter)`) per-scheme; the base class's three methods are templated over the descriptor. Same shape applied to opcodes: an `opcode_schema<Op>` descriptor that the daemon and the client both consume. **Subsumes #32 partially; compounds with #176, #177, #178, #181, #182, N3, N11, N23, N40 (per-scheme drift between URL parser allow-list and `allowedAttrs`). See also:** Cluster G, A6, U10. Effort: structural.

- **Validation:** VALID. N16. **Test boilerplate `JsonCharacterizationTest<T>` mirrors a missing production reflection.** The test-side `JsonCharacterizationTest<T>` and `VersionedProtoTest<Proto>` produce read+write tests from a `T` value plus a golden master file. The macro `VERSIONED_CHARACTERIZATION_TEST` writes 4 separate `TEST_F`s (`name_read`, `name_write`, `name_json_read`, `name_json_write`); independent grep returns 44 invocations across `libstore-tests/` (≈176 emitted tests; pass-1 said "130", pass-2 verifies ~134-176 depending on macro variant). The test infrastructure mirrors the missing production reflection exactly: `JsonCharacterizationTest<T>` is a (T × {read, write}) grid; `VersionedProtoTest<Proto>` is (Proto, T, Version) × {read, write}. The `*_NO_JSON` macro variant exists *because some types lack JSON serialisers* — a partial-coverage smell on the production side. Both templates are *evidence* that the pattern is mechanical and the implementations of `to_json`/`Serialise` are hand-coded boilerplate.
- **Validation:** VALID. Introduce `boost::describe` (header-only; cand. #9, U10), use it for the bulk of (T × format) pairs. **Compounds with #9 (JSON adl_serializer scaffolding); evidence for #176, #182 reflection refactoring. See also:** Cluster F, U10, N39 (test-data folder pattern), N41 (per-fixture `unitTestData` boilerplate). Effort: medium-large.

N17. **The "leaked Sync<T*>" idiom recurs three times for SIOF avoidance.** The codebase uses a leaked global Sync<T> pattern in three places to dodge static-init / dtor order issues: `getFileTransfer()` — `static auto * const _fileTransfer = new Sync<...>;` (#147); `windowSize` (terminal.cc) — leaked unique_ptr (#148, INVALID-as-fix; cleanup attempted-and-reverted); `getInterruptCallbacks()` — same hazard class. Plus the SIOF mitigation patterns: `Counter::enabled` static (counter.hh) reads `NIX_SHOW_STATS` once at startup; `nix::regex_init_once` (a `std::call_once` in `EvalState`'s ctor; #212). Pass-2 verified `_interruptCallbacks` is the third leaked-`Sync<T>` site. These are all manifestations of "process-wide singletons that need to outlive other process-wide singletons." Cand. #148 documents the hazard; #147 documents the fix direction; neither names that the *fix* is the same: don't use process-wide singletons when DI works (per-Store / per-EvalState reference); when DI is impractical (terminal, signals, logger), wrap the leak in a small `LeakedSingleton<T>` helper that documents the pattern and the rationale.
- **Validation:** VALID.
